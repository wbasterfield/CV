1. FACIAL RECOGNITION & COMPUTER VISION

Project context:

Daxicare / custom security and control-room platform

Warren built the complete software system around facial recognition and vehicle recognition.

Facial Pipeline

Incoming image

↓

↓

Face detection

↓

↓

ArcFace / ResNet100 ONNX

↓

Facial feature vector

↓

Qdrant vector database

↓

Cosine similarity search

↓

Similarity converted to percentage

↓

Configured threshold

↓

Control-room alarm

Technical Details

  • SCRFD 2.5G KPS ONNX was used for facial detection.
  • ArcFace / ResNet100 ONNX was used to extract facial features.
  • Facial vectors were stored in Qdrant.
  • Qdrant was used as the vector store for blacklisted faces.
  • Incoming faces were converted into vectors and searched against the stored vectors.
  • Warren used cosine similarity for matching.
  • The resulting similarity value was converted into a percentage for the application's matching logic.
  • A match above the configured 80% threshold generated an alarm and control-room notification.
  • Warren built the surrounding C# application, integration and control-room functionality.

Important Wording:

The 80% figure represents a cosine-similarity matching threshold. It should NOT be described as "80% facial-recognition accuracy."

Why Onnx?

Warren used ONNX because C# has a standard way of working with ONNX models, making model deployment within his C# applications practical.

Arcface / Resnet

Warren used the existing model as supplied. His role was integrating and deploying it to extract facial features for vector-based searches rather than training the model himself.

Result

Warren's system produced a better facial-recognition result for the client than the existing Dahua system could provide.

This is one of the AI projects Warren is most proud of.

4. PREDICTIVE MAINTENANCE / TIME-SERIES FORECASTING

Project:

Maintenance In Balance (MIB)

CMServices Global Ltd

2022–2023

Warren developed the software and technology side of MIB in partnership with Simon Edmondson, who supplied the vibration data and specialist machinery-health / vibration-analysis expertise.

Warren's technical contribution included:

  • Processing raw vibration waveform data
  • FFT-based signal processing
  • Converting waveform data into frequency bands
  • Bearing-specific analysis
  • Time-series forecasting
  • Predictive analysis
  • Web platform development
  • Reporting

The approach was:

Raw vibration waveform

↓

Fft

↓

Frequency bands

↓

Bearing-specific analysis

↓

Time-series forecasting

↓

Future trend / failure prediction

↓

Maintenance report

The system was designed to predict bearing problems and likely failure timing.

Approximately:

  • 1,000 bearings
  • 200 machines
  • 10 clients

Warren's biggest technical contribution was the approach to converting raw vibration waveform data into frequency-band information that could then be used for time-series forecasting.

During his research at the time, Warren was unable to identify an existing implementation that performed this particular transformation. He therefore developed his own approach.

Important:

Do not describe this as an independently verified "industry first." It was Warren's own approach based on the research available to him at the time.
