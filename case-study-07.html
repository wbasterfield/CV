The Partnership

Maintenance In Balance (MIB) was developed within CMServices Global Ltd, where Warren Basterfield was a technical partner. Simon Edmondson headed the machinery-health side of the business and provided the vibration data together with his specialist expertise in vibration analysis.

Warren brought the software and engineering capability. He designed and coded the complete software platform and translated Simon's machinery-health expertise into a working software system.

The Challenge

The objective was to use vibration measurements from monitored machinery to identify developing bearing problems and provide maintenance engineers with useful information about what was happening and when a failure was likely to occur.

The source measurements included waveform data stored as raw bytes. The challenge was to turn that raw signal data into information that could be analyzed in the context of specific bearings and then used to forecast future behavior.

Turning Vibration Data into Predictive Information

Based on Simon's vibration-analysis expertise, Warren identified a way to use signal processing and time-series forecasting to move from historical measurements toward a prediction of future events.

The processing pipeline included:

  • Raw vibration waveform data
  • Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) processing
  • Separation of the waveform into frequency bands
  • Analysis of relevant frequency bands using bearing-specific information and manufacturer specifications
  • Time-series forecasting using the resulting measurement trends
  • Forecasting future bearing behavior
  • Generation of maintenance information for engineering review

The FFT-based approach was Warren's own technical innovation within the project. During his research at the time, Warren was unable to identify an existing implementation that performed this particular transformation of raw vibration waveform data into frequency-band information for subsequent time-series forecasting. He therefore developed his own approach.

From Domain Expertise to Software

Simon provided the domain expertise required to interpret vibration behavior and supplied the underlying data. Warren translated that knowledge into software and mathematical processing.

Simon could then review the resulting predictions against his practical machinery-health experience and determine whether the results made engineering sense.

The partnership was essential to the product: Simon brought the machinery-health knowledge, while Warren engineered the technology required to turn that knowledge into a working predictive system.

Predicting Bearing Failure

The system was designed to predict when bearings were likely to fail.

New vibration measurements could be incorporated into the system so that the trends and forecasts could be updated as additional data became available.

The resulting output was intended to be useful to maintenance engineers rather than simply presenting an abstract AI score. Reports identified:

  • Which bearing was causing a problem
  • What about the bearing was causing the problem
  • When the bearing was likely to fail

Scale

The system covered approximately 1,000 bearings across 200 machines and 10 clients.

The Software Platform

Warren developed the complete software platform, including the data-processing and forecasting functionality and the web-based application used to present the resulting information.

The platform brought the machinery-health analysis together into a practical environment for reviewing equipment condition and predicted maintenance issues.

Technical Innovation

The most significant technical contribution was Warren's approach to converting raw vibration waveform data into frequency-band information that could then be used for time-series forecasting.

This created a bridge between established vibration-analysis knowledge and predictive software: the physical behavior of machinery could be represented as data, analyzed computationally, and projected forward to support maintenance decisions.

Commercial Product

MIB was developed as a commercial product within CMServices Global Ltd. Public CMServices material from April 2022 described the product as a machinery-health solution incorporating machine learning and artificial intelligence, with an emphasis on combining established condition-monitoring expertise with AI-assisted diagnostic capability.

The product was also demonstrated publicly, providing visual evidence of the software in operation.

In 2023, CMServices Global Ltd / Simon Edmondson received an Award of Excellence described in the supplied award correspondence as recognizing the business as a “Global Provider of Innovation Services in Machinery Health 2023.” The award correspondence identifies the recipient as CMServices Global Ltd / Simon Edmondson rather than Warren personally.

Why This Project Matters

MIB demonstrates Warren's ability to work across disciplines and turn specialist domain knowledge into an engineered technology platform.

He entered the project without prior machinery-health domain expertise, worked alongside a specialist who supplied the data and diagnostic knowledge, and developed the software, signal-processing approach and forecasting methodology needed to make that expertise usable computationally.

The result was an AI-assisted machinery-health platform that moved from raw vibration measurements to frequency analysis, forecasting and actionable maintenance information.
